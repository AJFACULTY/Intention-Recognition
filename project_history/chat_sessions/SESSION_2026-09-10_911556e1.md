### Why Did That Sound "Too Rigorous"?

You have hit on one of the biggest traps in academic and engineering writing! 

In academia, guides and reviewers often use heavy, intimidating terms like *"deterministic middleware coupling,"* *"mathematical invariance,"* and *"standards compliance."* When written poorly, this turns a thesis into a dry, robotic, impenetrable wall of text that is exhausting to read.

**Great engineering writing should be the exact opposite:**
- **Clear and Welcoming:** Anyone—whether it is an external examiner, a fellow engineering student, or an interested industry colleague—should be able to pick up the thesis, understand the story, and enjoy reading it.
- **Intuitive First, Technical Second:** We explain the *real-world common sense* first, and then follow up with the formula or metric.
  - *Stiff/Dense:* *"The spatial coordinate domain shift induces non-linear degradation of landmark classification tensors."*
  - *Friendly/Intuitive:* *"When a person steps two meters back from the robot, their hand appears much smaller in the camera frame. If our software only measures raw pixel positions, it gets confused and fails. To fix this, we measure the angles between fingers and proportional ratios instead of pixels—so a thumbs-up looks identical whether the person is standing close or across the room."*
- **A Relatable Engineering Journey:** It tells an honest, interesting story:
  1. *What we wanted the robot to do* (understand hand gestures and body movement without needing expensive cloud servers).
  2. *What surprised us or got in the way* (crowded rooms, changing distances, hardware limits).
  3. *The smart, practical choices we made to solve it*.
  4. *What we tested, what worked, and what we learned.*
