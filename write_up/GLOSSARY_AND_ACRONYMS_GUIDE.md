# Part II: Technical Glossary (Non-Everyday English Terms)

### 1. Robotics Kinematics, State Estimation & Odometry

#### **Kinematics**
- **Technical Definition:** The branch of classical mechanics that describes the motion of points, bodies, and systems of bodies without consideration of the forces that cause the motion. Divided into *Forward Kinematics* (calculating vehicle position and velocity from wheel rotation rates) and *Inverse Kinematics* (calculating required wheel rotation rates to achieve a desired vehicle velocity vector).
- **In This Project:** When `brain_node` issues a velocity command of $0.25\,\text{m/s}$ linear and $0.0\,\text{rad/s}$ angular on `/cmd_vel`, the ESP32-S3 uses inverse kinematics equations for a differential-drive chassis to calculate the exact revolutions per minute (RPM) for the left and right wheel motor pairs.
- **Analogy:** Calculating how far your bicycle traveled simply by counting how many times the rubber wheel spun around, without caring about how hard you pushed the pedals.

#### **Non-Holonomic Constraint**
- **Technical Definition:** A kinematic constraint on a mechanical system where the controllable degrees of freedom are fewer than the total degrees of freedom of the configuration space. Specifically, a mobile vehicle cannot instantaneously accelerate sideways without first steering or turning its chassis ($\dot{y}_{\text{local}} = 0$).
- **In This Project:** The Yahboom 4WD mobile robot is non-holonomic; it can translate forward/backward and rotate in place, but cannot slide sideways like a crab or an omnidirectional mecanum wheel robot. Nav2 planners must respect this physical limit.
- **Analogy:** A standard automobile. You cannot slide directly sideways into a parallel parking spot; you must turn the steering wheel and pull forward or backward along a curve.

#### **Odometry & Dead-Reckoning**
- **Technical Definition:** The mathematical process of estimating the instantaneous change in a vehicle's position $(x, y)$ and orientation ($\theta$) over time by integrating motion sensor data, primarily wheel encoder tick counts and inertial turn rates.
- **In This Project:** The ESP32-S3 reads high-resolution optical quadrature encoders attached to the motor shafts. By multiplying tick deltas by wheel circumference and dividing by gear ratios, it integrates incremental distance traveled at $11.5\,\text{Hz}$, publishing raw odometry on `/odom_raw`.
- **Analogy:** Walking across a dark field with your eyes shut, estimating where you are solely by counting your physical footsteps and keeping track of how many times you turned your shoulders.

#### **Quaternion**
- **Technical Definition:** A four-dimensional complex number system of the form $q = w + xi + yj + zk$ (where $w, x, y, z \in \mathbb{R}$ and $i^2 = j^2 = k^2 = ijk = -1$) used in robotics and computer graphics to represent 3D orientations without suffering from *Gimbal Lock* (a mathematical singularity encountered when using 3-axis Euler angles Pitch, Roll, and Yaw where one degree of freedom is lost).
- **In This Project:** All ROS 2 pose messages (`geometry_msgs/Pose`, `/amcl_pose`, TF transforms) represent the robot's heading using unit quaternions. Because the mobile robot operates on a 2D floor plane, pitch and roll are zero, and planar yaw $\theta$ is extracted using: $\theta = 2 \cdot \text{atan2}(q_z, q_w)$.
- **Analogy:** A mathematical compass needle that can point anywhere in 3D space without ever jamming or getting caught in an impossible mechanical knot.

#### **Coordinate Frames & Transform Tree (TF2)**
- **Technical Definition:** Spatial Cartesian coordinate systems $(X, Y, Z)$ attached to specific rigid physical links or reference origins. The ROS TF2 library maintains a directed tree structure of geometric transformations between these frames, tracking time-stamped translation vectors and rotation quaternions.
- **In This Project:** The transform tree links the world together: `map` $\to$ `odom` (corrected by AMCL) $\to$ `base_footprint` (ground projection) $\to$ `base_link` (robot axle center) $\to$ `laser_frame` (LiDAR sensor) and `camera_link` (monocular camera).
- **Analogy:** Directions relative to different landmarks: "The cup is 2 feet from your chest, your chest is 5 feet from the door, and the door is 20 feet from the street." TF2 handles the continuous math to tell you where the cup is relative to the street.

#### **Covariance Matrix**
- **Technical Definition:** A square matrix whose diagonal elements represent the statistical variances of multiple estimated variables, and whose off-diagonal elements represent the pairwise covariances (degree of mutual linear dependency) between them.
- **In This Project:** Both `/odom_raw` and `/amcl_pose` include a $6 \times 6$ covariance matrix that tells the EKF and navigation stack how much statistical confidence to place in the $(x, y, \theta)$ position estimates.
- **Analogy:** When you tell a friend, "I am 99% confident I am in the kitchen, but only 50% confident I am facing North." The covariance matrix is the mathematical table of those confidence percentages.

#### **Yaw Drift**
- **Technical Definition:** The gradual, unbounded accumulation of angular orientation error over time when integrating gyroscope angular velocity ($\omega_z$) due to micro-electro-mechanical (MEMS) sensor bias, thermal fluctuations, and numerical integration errors.
- **In This Project:** In early testing, raw IMU integration caused the robot's estimated heading to drift by several degrees per minute. This was resolved by fusing wheel odometry with the EKF and relying on SLAM Toolbox / AMCL scan-matching against static map walls to periodically eliminate orientation drift.
- **Analogy:** A steering wheel alignment that pulls very slightly to the right; if you drive for 5 miles without looking out the window, you will drift completely off the highway.

### 2. Environmental Mapping & Autonomous Navigation

#### **Occupancy Grid**
- **Technical Definition:** A discrete spatial tessellation of an indoor environment into a uniform 2D grid of cells (typically $0.05\,\text{m} \times 0.05\,\text{m}$ resolution). Each cell contains a scalar integer probability value representing whether that spatial patch is open free space (0), completely occupied by a wall/obstacle (100), or unobserved/unknown (-1).
- **In This Project:** Generated live by `slam_toolbox` and saved as `room_map_20260812_0826.yaml` and `.png`. Nav2 paths are calculated exclusively through cells having an occupancy value of 0.
- **Analogy:** A sheet of graph paper covering your living room floor where every square is colored white (walkable floor), black (couch/wall), or grey (behind closed doors).

#### **Costmap (Global and Local)**
- **Technical Definition:** A multi-layered 2D scalar potential grid generated by Nav2 where numerical cost values ($0 \to 254$) are assigned to spatial cells. Costs increase exponentially as cells approach physical obstacles.
  - **Global Costmap:** Covers the entire facility blueprint; built from the static occupancy grid to calculate the long-distance global path.
  - **Local Costmap:** A smaller, rolling window ($3.0\,\text{m} \times 3.0\,\text{m}$) centered dynamically on the robot that ingests real-time LiDAR scans to detect moving humans and temporary obstacles.
- **In This Project:** When a person steps in front of the patrolling robot, the local costmap instantly inflates an obstacle at their feet, forcing the local trajectory controller (DWB) to slow down or navigate around them.
- **Analogy:** A heat map where walls and human legs glow burning red hot. The robot acts like an ice cube, naturally sliding along the coolest paths where costs are lowest.

#### **Inflation Layer & Cost Scaling Factor**
- **Technical Definition:** A software plugin within the Nav2 costmap pipeline that artificially expands the boundary of detected physical obstacles by adding an *inflation radius* ($r_{\text{inflation}}$) and decaying cost gradient governed by a exponential cost scaling factor ($\alpha$).
- **In This Project:** The robot's inflation radius is set to **0.25 m** (larger than the robot's physical radius of 0.15 m). This guarantees that Nav2 path planners maintain a minimum safety cushion between the chassis and physical walls.
- **Analogy:** Putting a soft, invisible foam bumper ring around every wall and table so you never brush your clothes against wet paint.

### 3. Computer Vision, Deep Learning & Artificial Intelligence

#### **Geometric Feature Extraction (19-D Feature Vector)**
- **Technical Definition:** A feature engineering method wherein raw spatial pixel coordinates are mathematically transformed into scale-invariant, rotation-tolerant geometric primitives (Euclidean distances normalized by palm breadth, inter-phalangeal angles, and joint curl metrics).
- **In This Project:** Instead of feeding 42 raw $(x, y)$ pixel coordinates into the neural network (which change drastically if the user steps forward or backward), our pipeline computes **19 scale-invariant geometric relationships** (e.g., thumb curl angle, fingertip-to-wrist ratios). This achieved **99.38% classification accuracy** across all distances.
- **Analogy:** Identifying a person by the ratio of their arm length to their torso height, rather than checking how tall their silhouette appears in a distant photograph.

#### **Domain Shift (Covariate Shift)**
- **Technical Definition:** The statistical phenomenon where the input data distribution $P(X)$ encountered during real-world physical operation differs from the training distribution $P(X)$ used to train the machine learning model, despite the underlying conditional label mapping $P(Y|X)$ remaining unchanged.
- **In This Project:** A classifier trained on web images taken by a photographer standing still will fail when deployed on a vibrating mobile robot with a wide-angle lens at floor level. We eliminated domain shift by collecting all 6,000 training samples directly through the robot's onboard CMOS camera in its actual operating environment.
- **Analogy:** Training an athlete to run exclusively on a smooth indoor treadmill, and then expecting them to run a marathon through muddy mountain trails without slipping.

#### **Multilayer Perceptron (MLP)**
- **Technical Definition:** A class of feedforward artificial neural network architecture consisting of at least three layers of nodes (an input layer, one or more hidden non-linear transformation layers, and an output layer). Nodes in adjacent layers are fully connected via learnable synaptic weights and biases.
- **In This Project:** A compact architecture: **19 Inputs $\to$ Dense(64, ReLU) $\to$ Dropout(0.2) $\to$ Dense(32, ReLU) $\to$ Dense(6, Softmax)**. Weighs only **46 KB** and evaluates in **1.2 ms** on the Raspberry Pi 5 CPU.
- **Analogy:** A three-stage committee of inspectors. The first 64 inspectors check raw hand measurements, the next 32 combine those insights, and the final 6 vote on which gesture was displayed.

#### **Softmax Activation Function**
- **Technical Definition:** A mathematical function that converts a vector of $K$ real-valued numbers (logits $z_i$) into a normalized probability distribution of $K$ probabilities proportional to the exponentials of the input numbers:
  $$\sigma(\mathbf{z})_i = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_{j=1}^K e^{z_j}} \quad \text{for } i = 1, \dots, K$$
- **In This Project:** The final layer of the MLP uses Softmax so the output sums to exactly $1.0$ ($100\%$), allowing `brain_node` to enforce an unambiguous confidence threshold ($>0.75$) before accepting any gesture.
- **Analogy:** Converting raw referee point scores into percentages so you can declare, "I am 94% sure this is a STOP command, 4% sure it is GO, and 2% sure it is FOLLOW."

#### **Vanishing Gradient Problem & Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)**
- **Technical Definition:** During backpropagation through deep recurrent neural networks, gradient signals are repeatedly multiplied by recurrent weight matrices. If eigenvalues are less than 1.0, the gradients diminish exponentially toward zero, preventing earlier time-steps from updating their weights. LSTMs prevent this by introducing an additive *Constant Error Carousel* (CEC) regulated by input, forget, and output gates.
- **In This Project:** Predicting human motion requires analyzing sequential body positions over 10 consecutive frames (~1.0 second). The LSTM preserves early movement cues without vanishing gradients, achieving an ADE of **25.8 px**.
- **Analogy:** Playing a game of "telephone" where the message is whispered down a line of 20 people; in a basic RNN, the message fades to silence by person 10. In an LSTM, each person carries a notebook and writes down the exact keywords to pass them forward intact.

### 4. Distributed Systems & Middleware Architecture

#### **Data Distribution Service (DDS) & Peer-to-Peer Bus**
- **Technical Definition:** An open, real-time middleware protocol defined by the Object Management Group (OMG) that manages high-throughput, low-latency, decentralized data exchange between distributed publishers and subscribers using a shared global data space without a central server or message broker.
- **In This Project:** ROS 2 Humble uses DDS (`rmw_fastrtps_cpp`). Unlike ROS 1 which relied on a single `roscore` master (a single point of failure), ROS 2 nodes discover each other automatically via multicast on **Domain ID 20**. If any vision node crashes, the motor safety node continues executing unaffected.
- **Analogy:** A group of specialists wearing walkie-talkies tuned to Channel 20. If one doctor faints, the security guard and paramedic can still talk to each other without interruption.

#### **Quality of Service (QoS) Profiles**
- **Technical Definition:** Declarative contract parameters negotiated between DDS publishers and subscribers that dictate reliability, history depth, liveliness, and durability of message transmission.
  - **RELIABLE:** Guarantees message delivery via automatic acknowledgment and retransmission packets (TCP-like behavior).
  - **BEST_EFFORT:** Transmits packets without delivery confirmation, discarding packets if buffers saturate (UDP-like behavior).
  - **TRANSIENT_LOCAL:** Latches the most recent message so that newly created late-joining nodes immediately receive the last published state.
- **In This Project:** Motor commands (`/cmd_vel`) and navigation goals (`/amcl_pose`) use **RELIABLE** and **TRANSIENT_LOCAL** to prevent missed safety halts. High-frequency video (`/camera/image_raw/compressed`) and LiDAR (`/scan`) use **BEST_EFFORT** to prevent memory buffer bloat.
- **Analogy:** Choosing between registered courier delivery for legal contracts (Reliable) vs. watching a streaming football match where dropping a single video frame is better than freezing the screen for 10 seconds (Best Effort).

#### **Micro-ROS (Client and Agent)**
- **Technical Definition:** An embedded software framework that extends ROS 2 into resource-constrained microcontrollers running real-time operating systems (RTOS). It utilizes the Micro XRCE-DDS (eXtremely Resource Constrained Environments) protocol over serial, UART, or SPI buses.
  - **Micro-ROS Client:** Lightweight C firmware running on the ESP32-S3 microcontroller under FreeRTOS.
  - **Micro-ROS Agent:** A gateway node running under Linux on the Raspberry Pi 5 that translates compact Micro XRCE-DDS serial packets into standard DDS messages on the main ROS 2 graph.
- **In This Project:** The client runs on the ESP32-S3 co-processor, transmitting encoder ticks and IMU data across a dedicated **921,600 baud UART** bus to the agent on the Pi 5, maintaining a rock-solid communication loop with zero Linux OS scheduling interference.
- **Analogy:** A multilingual interpreter. The ESP32 speaks a fast, compact shorthand dialect; the Micro-ROS Agent translates that shorthand into formal English so the rest of the computer network understands it instantly.

#### **Twist Multiplexer (twist_mux) & Priority Arbitration**
- **Technical Definition:** A ROS software node that multiplexes multiple velocity input topics (`geometry_msgs/Twist`) into a single output velocity topic (`/cmd_vel`) based on a deterministic, priority-ordered lock schedule with automatic topic timeouts.
- **In This Project:** Arbitrates robot motion across four priority layers:
  1. **Emergency E-Stop (`/cmd_vel_e_stop`):** Priority **100** (Highest — instant halt).
  2. **Joystick Manual Teleoperation (`/cmd_vel_joy`):** Priority **50** (Operator override).
  3. **Cognitive Gesture Commands (`/cmd_vel_gesture`):** Priority **40** (Touchless human directives).
  4. **Autonomous Nav2 Navigation (`/cmd_vel_nav`):** Priority **20** (Lowest — background patrol).
- **Analogy:** An emergency ambulance siren on a highway. Normal traffic (Nav2) yields immediately when an authorized emergency vehicle (Gesture or E-Stop) demands the road.

### 5. Collaborative Robotics & Safety Standards

#### **ISO 15066:2016 (Speed and Separation Monitoring)**
- **Technical Definition:** An international technical specification governing collaborative industrial robot systems and the work environment. Section 5.5.4 establishes the mathematical formulation for *Speed and Separation Monitoring*, requiring that the protective separation distance ($S$) between a human and robot never drop below the distance the robot requires to come to a complete physical halt:
  $$S = (v_r \cdot T_r) + (v_h \cdot T_h) + B_r + C$$
  Where $v_r$ is robot speed, $T_r$ is robot reaction time, $v_h$ is human approach speed, $T_h$ is sensor latency, $B_r$ is physical braking distance, and $C$ is the intrusion clearance margin.
- **In This Project:** With an end-to-end perception latency budget of **74.2 ms**, vehicle speed of $0.25\,\text{m/s}$, and maximum braking deceleration of $0.8\,\text{m/s}^2$, the robot achieves a safe physical stop in **0.42 m**, well within the configured **0.45 m** LiDAR safety clearance threshold.
- **Analogy:** The safe following distance rule when driving: you must always leave enough car lengths in front of you so that if the car ahead slams its brakes, your reaction time and braking distance will stop you before any impact occurs.
