\chapter{Conclusion and Recommendation}

\section{Summary of the Study}
This research set out to design, implement, and empirically evaluate an edge-deployed robotic system capable of recognizing human intentions through hand gestures and body movement, translating them into deterministic autonomous navigation commands without reliance on external cloud computing or power-intensive graphics processors. The physical platform was developed on a Yahboom differential-drive chassis controlled by an embedded Raspberry Pi 5 edge processor and an ESP32-S3 microcontroller running micro-ROS. By combining spatial person filtering, MediaPipe anatomical hand tracking, scale-invariant geometric feature engineering, an ONNX-optimized Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), and an LSTM movement trajectory predictor, the system established a robust, touchless human-robot interaction interface.

Across the lifecycle of the investigation, six primary specific objectives were pursued and successfully realized:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A multi-stage vision perception pipeline was implemented natively on the Raspberry Pi 5 CPU, integrating a central spatial acceptance zone, MediaPipe skeletal joint extraction, and 19 engineered geometric hand features.
    \item A custom, balanced dataset of 6,000 gesture samples across six discrete commands (STOP, GO, LEFT, RIGHT, BACK, FOLLOW) was gathered directly through the onboard monocular camera, completely eliminating sensor domain shift errors.
    \item An LSTM recurrent neural network was developed and evaluated across 152 empirical motion sequences to anticipate short-horizon operator trajectories from kinematic pose histories.
    \item A deterministic decision state machine (\texttt{brain\_node}) was implemented within a multi-container ROS2 Humble software architecture, incorporating a 5-frame rolling majority buffer, a 3.0-second operator lock, and proportional visual servoing.
    \item Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) was configured and physically validated on the mobile platform, producing clean metric occupancy grids following the resolution of Extended Kalman Filter transform race conditions and yaw drift.
    \item Comprehensive empirical benchmarking was conducted, validating system performance through classification metrics, confusion matrices, component latency budgets, and embedded processor resource utilization.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
The experimental results demonstrate that multi-modal human intention recognition can be achieved natively on resource-constrained embedded edge hardware while sustaining the low latency required for safe collaborative robotics. The Multilayer Perceptron classifier, trained on 19 scale- and distance-invariant geometric features, achieved an unseen test accuracy of 99.38\% with balanced precision and recall ($\ge 0.987$) across all six operational classes. Physical interaction trials conducted with three independent human participants yielded an aggregate mean recognition accuracy of 96.67\% across 180 trials, confirming that the engineered geometric features successfully eliminate perspective distortion and distance-induced domain shift.

Furthermore, the system demonstrated robust real-time responsiveness on the embedded hardware. Cumulative latency profiling revealed an end-to-end sensing-to-actuation cycle of 132.0~ms, well below the standard 150--250~ms human visual-motor reaction threshold established in collaborative robotics standards. The centralized decision node executed velocity commands within 96--124~ms, enforced reactive safety stops at 0.36~m obstacle clearances, and sustained an aggregate CPU load of 77.8\% per core on the Raspberry Pi 5 with over 3.9~GB of memory headroom. 

Physical deployment surfaced and resolved critical engineering bottlenecks that could not be diagnosed in virtual simulation alone, including out-of-memory kernel aborts under 2GB RAM configurations, transform race conditions causing LiDAR scan drops, and Extended Kalman Filter heading drift. By combining active gesture directives with passive motion prediction and physical SLAM mapping, this research successfully bridges the gap between fast intention recognition and mobile robotic navigation, delivering a scalable, GPU-independent paradigm for Human-Robot Interaction in shared industrial and healthcare workspaces.

\section{Limitations of the Study}
While the experimental findings validate the functional feasibility of the deployed system, several technical constraints and limitations should be recognized:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Participant Sample Size and Demographic Diversity:} Physical interaction trials were conducted with three participants performing ten trials per gesture class (180 physical trials total). While this sample size proved sufficient for functional verification, a larger and more demographically varied participant cohort is required to establish comprehensive statistical epidemiological generalization across wider anatomical variations and motor proficiencies.
    \item \textbf{Sensitivity to Ambient Illumination:} The monocular camera pipeline relies on passive optical reflectance. Under low ambient lighting ($< 80$ lux), camera sensor gain introduced pixel noise that degraded MediaPipe landmark extraction confidence, causing occasional tracking dropouts. The system remains dependent on adequate indoor lighting ($> 150$ lux) for optimal performance.
    \item \textbf{Autonomous Navigation Deployment Constraints:} While physical SLAM occupancy grid mapping was validated, autonomous waypoint navigation using the complete Nav2 stack in dynamic, populated physical environments encountered embedded computational limits. The concurrent execution of global costmaps, local planners, and heavy perception models pushed CPU limits, leaving full closed-loop autonomous navigation as an area for ongoing refinement.
    \item \textbf{Offline Biometric Operator Tracking:} A facial recognition prototype utilizing InsightFace (MobileFaceNet) was successfully evaluated offline, distinguishing enrolled operators with confidence scores of 0.54--0.57 against an unknown baseline of 0.09. However, this node was not integrated into the live containerized pipeline due to Python library constraints on the embedded image, leaving operator identity tracking reliant on geometric spatial-zone heuristics.
    \item \textbf{Visual Servoing Damping Dynamics:} The proportional visual servoing controller ($K_p = 1.5$) executed reactive person tracking based on instantaneous horizontal centroid error. When operators made rapid lateral maneuvers, the absence of derivative damping or integral error accumulation resulted in minor vehicle heading oscillations before stabilizing.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Recommendations for Future Work}
Based on the experimental outcomes and operational limitations identified during this research, the following technical extensions are recommended for future investigations:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Upgrade to Closed-Loop PID and Kalman-Filtered Servoing:} The current proportional controller should be upgraded to a tuned Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) controller incorporating a predictive Kalman filter on the operator's centroid trajectory. This enhancement would provide derivative damping, eliminating heading oscillations and enabling smoother vehicle trajectories during rapid human maneuvers.
    \item \textbf{Independent Active Gimbal Tracking:} Integrating autonomous pan-tilt servo tracking decoupled from chassis locomotion would allow the camera to center the human operator independently of mobile base rotation. This would maintain visual tracking lock even when the mobile robot is stationary or executing independent obstacle-avoidance maneuvers.
    \item \textbf{Embedded Deployment of Quantized Biometric Identity Models:} The offline facial recognition prototype should be quantized to INT8 precision and integrated into the live containerized pipeline via TensorRT or ONNX Runtime. Fusing facial identity embeddings with spatial-zone filtering would provide persistent operator authentication, eliminating identity confusion when multiple individuals enter the operational zone.
    \item \textbf{Serialized SLAM State and Multi-Session Mapping:} SLAM Toolbox should be configured to save serialized topological session states rather than flattened image exports. This would allow the mobile platform to resume previous mapping sessions, perform global loop-closure optimization, and incrementally refine spatial maps across long-term deployments.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Sensor Perception Fusion (RGB-D / Ultrasonic):} Incorporating low-power solid-state depth cameras or ultrasonic proximity arrays would provide redundant distance measurements, mitigating vision degradation under adverse illumination conditions and enhancing near-field obstacle detection below the LiDAR planar beam.
    \item \textbf{Industrial Safety Standard Formalization:} Future commercial deployments should incorporate safety-certified hardware programmable logic controllers (PLCs) compliant with ISO 13849-1 Performance Level d (PLd), providing hardware-level failsafe cutoffs independent of software operating systems.
\end{enumerate}
